# Everyday Swedish — preparation material

## How to study a section

1. Read the Swedish dialogue aloud once without looking at the translation.
2. Check only the lines you did not understand.
3. Cover the Swedish column and rebuild each line from the English.
4. Read the **Grammar** notes and say the examples aloud.
5. In the lesson, answer new questions with the same patterns: the teacher
   changes names, places, prices and days on purpose.

Swedish uses **du** with almost everyone, so the whole track does.

# Session 1 — Introductions and small talk

## Dialogue 1A — At a neighbour's party

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| — Hej! Vad heter du? | — Hi! What's your name? |
| — Jag heter Marco. Och du? | — My name is Marco. And you? |
| — Jag heter Karin. Trevligt att träffas. | — I'm Karin. Nice to meet you. |
| — Detsamma. | — Likewise. |
| — Var kommer du ifrån? | — Where are you from? |
| — Jag kommer från Italien, men jag bor i Göteborg nu. | — I'm from Italy, but I live in Gothenburg now. |
| — Vad jobbar du med? | — What do you do for a living? |
| — Jag jobbar som programmerare. Och du? | — I work as a programmer. And you? |
| — Jag är lärare. | — I'm a teacher. |
| — Talar du svenska? | — Do you speak Swedish? |
| — Lite grann. Jag lär mig. | — A little. I'm learning. |

## Dialogue 1B — When you do not understand

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| — Hur länge har du bott här? | — How long have you lived here? |
| — Förlåt, jag förstår inte. Kan du prata lite långsammare? | — Sorry, I don't understand. Can you speak a bit more slowly? |
| — Hur länge … har du bott … här? | — How long … have you lived … here? |
| — Aha. I två år. | — Oh. For two years. |
| — Trivs du? | — Do you like it here? |
| — Vad betyder «trivs»? | — What does “trivs” mean? |
| — Att man mår bra och tycker om ett ställe. | — That you feel good and like a place. |
| — Ja, jag trivs jättebra! Hur säger man «neighbour» på svenska? | — Yes, I like it a lot! How do you say “neighbour” in Swedish? |
| — Granne. | — Granne. |

## Core patterns

- Jag heter … / Vad heter du? — My name is … / What's your name?
- Jag kommer från … / Var kommer du ifrån? — I'm from … / Where are you from?
- Jag bor i … — I live in …
- Jag jobbar som … / Jag är … — I work as a … / I am a … (no article: *Jag är lärare*)
- Jag talar … och lite svenska. — I speak … and a little Swedish.

## Repair expressions

- Förlåt, jag förstår inte. — Sorry, I don't understand.
- Kan du säga det igen? — Can you say that again?
- Kan du prata lite långsammare? — Can you speak a bit more slowly?
- Vad betyder …? — What does … mean?
- Hur säger man … på svenska? — How do you say … in Swedish?
- Kan du stava det? — Can you spell it?
- Vänta lite. — Wait a moment.

## Grammar — present tense and verb-second order

The present tense ends in **-r** and is the same for every person:
*jag bor, du bor, hon bor, vi bor*. Questions put the verb first (*Bor du
här?*) or right after a question word (*Var bor du?*).

| Infinitive | Present | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| att heta | heter | Jag heter Marco. |
| att komma | kommer | Hon kommer från Spanien. |
| att bo | bor | Vi bor i Malmö. |
| att jobba | jobbar | Han jobbar som läkare. |
| att tala | talar | Talar du engelska? |
| att vara | är | Jag är lärare. |

Drill ideas: statement → yes/no question (*Du bor här. → Bor du här?*);
change the person (*jag bor → vi bor*: unchanged verb); answer with a new
detail (*Var bor du? — i Uppsala*).

## Vocabulary

- ett land — a country; en stad — a city
- en granne — a neighbour
- en kollega — a colleague
- ett jobb — a job; att jobba — to work
- en lärare, en läkare, en programmerare — a teacher, a doctor, a programmer
- att förstå — to understand
- långsamt / långsammare — slowly / more slowly
- att trivas — to feel at home, to like it (somewhere)
- lite grann — a little
- Trevligt att träffas. — Nice to meet you.
- Detsamma. — Likewise.

## Writing

Write four or five sentences introducing yourself to a new neighbour on the
building's notice board: your name, where you come from, where you work, and
one thing you like about the area. Follow-up: add one question for the
neighbours.

## You should be able to

- [ ] Say your name, where you are from and where you live.
- [ ] Say what you do and which languages you speak.
- [ ] Ask the same questions back.
- [ ] Ask for repetition, slower speech and the meaning of a word.

# Session 2 — Fika, food and likes

## Dialogue 2A — At the café

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| — Hej, vad får det lov att vara? | — Hi, what can I get you? |
| — Jag skulle vilja ha en kaffe, tack. | — I'd like a coffee, please. |
| — Bryggkaffe eller latte? | — Filter coffee or latte? |
| — Bryggkaffe. Har ni kanelbullar? | — Filter coffee. Do you have cinnamon buns? |
| — Ja, och kardemummabullar. | — Yes, and cardamom buns. |
| — En kanelbulle också, tack. Vad kostar det? | — A cinnamon bun too, please. How much is it? |
| — Det blir sextiofem kronor. | — That's sixty-five kronor. |
| — Kan jag betala med kort? | — Can I pay by card? |
| — Ja, visst. Varsågod. | — Yes, of course. Here you are. |
| — Tack! | — Thanks! |

## Dialogue 2B — What do you like?

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| — Tycker du om kaffe? | — Do you like coffee? |
| — Ja, jag tycker om svart kaffe. Och du? | — Yes, I like black coffee. And you? |
| — Jag dricker mest te. | — I mostly drink tea. |
| — Jag gillar inte te så mycket. | — I don't like tea that much. |
| — Vad brukar du äta till frukost? | — What do you usually eat for breakfast? |
| — Jag brukar äta yoghurt och en smörgås. | — I usually have yoghurt and a sandwich. |
| — Ska vi fika i morgon? | — Shall we have a fika tomorrow? |
| — Ja, gärna! | — Yes, I'd love to! |

## Core patterns

- Jag skulle vilja ha … , tack. — I'd like … , please.
- Har ni …? — Do you have …?
- Vad kostar …? / Det blir … kronor. — How much is …? / That's … kronor.
- Jag tycker om … / Jag gillar … / Jag gillar inte … — I like … / I don't like …
- Jag brukar … — I usually …
- Ska vi …? — Shall we …?

## Grammar — en and ett, and the definite form

Every noun is **en** or **ett**; learn it with the word. The definite form
adds the article at the end: **-en / -n** for en-words, **-et / -t** for
ett-words.

| Indefinite | Definite | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| en bulle | bullen | the bun |
| en kopp | koppen | the cup |
| en smörgås | smörgåsen | the sandwich |
| ett kafé | kaféet | the café |
| ett glas | glaset | the glass |
| ett äpple | äpplet | the apple |
| ett bord | bordet | the table |

Drill ideas: indefinite → definite (*en kopp → koppen*); add *inte* after the
verb (*Jag gillar te. → Jag gillar inte te.*).

## Vocabulary

- en kaffe, ett te, en latte — a coffee, a tea, a latte
- en kanelbulle — a cinnamon bun
- en smörgås — a sandwich
- ett glas vatten — a glass of water
- mjölk, socker — milk, sugar
- en krona, kronor — a krona, kronor
- att kosta — to cost
- att betala — to pay; med kort / kontant — by card / in cash
- att fika — to have coffee and a bite with someone
- att tycka om, att gilla — to like
- att bruka + verb (*jag brukar äta*) — to usually do
- gärna — gladly, I'd love to

## Writing

Write five sentences about your perfect fika: where, with whom, what you
drink and eat, and what you do not like. Follow-up: rewrite two of the
sentences with *brukar*.

## You should be able to

- [ ] Order a drink and something to eat, and ask what it costs.
- [ ] Understand a spoken price and pay by card.
- [ ] Say what you like and do not like.
- [ ] Suggest a fika.

# Session 3 — The weekend and plans

## Dialogue 3A — Monday morning

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| — Hej! Vad gjorde du i helgen? | — Hi! What did you do at the weekend? |
| — I fredags var jag hemma och vilade. | — On Friday I stayed at home and rested. |
| — Och i lördags? | — And on Saturday? |
| — I lördags gick jag på bio med en kollega. | — On Saturday I went to the cinema with a colleague. |
| — Vad såg ni? | — What did you see? |
| — En svensk film. Jag förstod nästan allt! | — A Swedish film. I understood almost everything! |
| — Och i söndags? | — And on Sunday? |
| — I söndags åt vi middag hos vänner. | — On Sunday we had dinner at friends'. |

## Dialogue 3B — Making plans

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| — Vad ska du göra nästa helg? | — What are you doing next weekend? |
| — Jag ska åka till Stockholm. | — I'm going to Stockholm. |
| — Vad ska du göra där? | — What are you going to do there? |
| — Jag ska träffa en kompis och gå på museum. Har du lust att följa med? | — I'm meeting a friend and going to a museum. Do you feel like coming along? |
| — Tyvärr kan jag inte. Jag ska jobba på lördag. | — Unfortunately I can't. I'm working on Saturday. |
| — Synd! Nästa gång kanske. | — Pity! Maybe next time. |
| — Ja, gärna. Vi hörs! | — Yes, I'd like that. Talk soon! |

## Core patterns

- Vad gjorde du i helgen? — What did you do at the weekend?
- I lördags gick jag … — On (last) Saturday I went …
- Vad ska du göra …? / Jag ska … — What are you going to do …? / I'm going to …
- Har du lust att …? — Do you feel like …?
- Ja, gärna! / Tyvärr kan jag inte. — Yes, I'd love to! / Unfortunately I can't.

## Grammar — the past tense, and the verb in second place

The past tense of common verbs is irregular; learn it as a pair.

| Present | Past | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| är | var | am → was |
| gör | gjorde | do → did |
| går | gick | go (walk) → went |
| äter | åt | eat → ate |
| ser | såg | see → saw |
| åker | åkte | travel → travelled |
| vilar | vilade | rest → rested |
| träffar | träffade | meet → met |

When a sentence starts with a time expression, the verb stays in **second
place** and the subject comes after it: *Jag gick på bio i lördags.* →
*I lördags gick jag på bio.*

The future is often **ska + infinitive**: *Jag ska åka till Stockholm.*

Drill ideas: present → past (*Jag äter middag. → Jag åt middag.*); move the
time expression to the front (*Jag var hemma i fredags. → I fredags var jag
hemma.*); past → plan with *ska* (*Jag åkte till Malmö. → Jag ska åka till
Malmö.*).

## Vocabulary

- helgen, i helgen — the weekend, at the weekend
- i fredags, i lördags, i söndags — last Friday, Saturday, Sunday
- på lördag — (this coming) Saturday
- nästa helg — next weekend
- att vila — to rest
- att gå på bio / på museum — to go to the cinema / to a museum
- middag — dinner
- hos vänner — at friends' (place)
- en kompis — a friend (informal)
- att följa med — to come along
- tyvärr — unfortunately
- Synd! — Pity!

## Writing

Write five or six sentences about last weekend, starting at least two of them
with a time expression (*I lördags …*). Follow-up: write two sentences about
what you are going to do next weekend, with *ska*.

## You should be able to

- [ ] Say what you did at the weekend, day by day.
- [ ] Ask someone else about their weekend.
- [ ] Say what you are going to do and invite someone along.
- [ ] Accept or decline an invitation.
