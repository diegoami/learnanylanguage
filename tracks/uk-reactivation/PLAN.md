# Review plan

## Preparation

Before each voice session, study the same-numbered section in
`MATERIAL.md`. Read the complete dialogues aloud, check the English
only where necessary, and then reconstruct the Ukrainian from the English.

The sample dialogues are preparation, not scripts to memorize. The voice teacher should test
the same communicative functions with changed details and follow-up questions.
Session 0 may be attempted cold first if a clean diagnostic baseline is wanted;
otherwise review its repair-language section before starting.

## General session shape

A session is worked through as several short lessons (about 5–10 minutes
each). Every lesson has exactly one mode:

1. **Repetition:** hear and repeat the session's complete authoritative
   opening, one line at a time, then a short recall pass in which each prompt
   is attempted first and then answered by the model line.
2. **Guided conversation:** one lesson part, one situation, four to six
   closely related functions, at most ten steps, changing only one familiar
   detail at a time. The learner attempts before any model.
3. **Free conversation:** an open conversation on the session topic at the
   learner's level, with no target list and only light, natural recasts.

Every lesson ends neutrally and hands off the raw transcript. Voice does not
score or summarize the learner. The usual order for a session is repetition,
then one guided lesson per part, then free conversation; any mode may be
repeated with changed details.

The assistant should test communication, not the learner's ability to explain
grammar terminology. Correct Ukrainian usage is the goal. New vocabulary must
be taught before it is required.

## Progression and diagnostic routing

Session 0 is a non-gating baseline. After one usable diagnostic transcript,
record remaining gaps as **Revisit** items and proceed to Session 1. Do not keep
repeating a broad diagnostic until every target passes.

Sessions 1–5 may be divided into focused parts. Each part teaches and practises
only four to six closely related expressions.

**Covered** means the session's topic areas have appeared in the accumulated
transcripts of any mode. Modelled, repeated, cued, corrected, and independent
turns all count as coverage. Covered does not mean mastered.

Never repeat a whole Covered session because some expressions remain weak.
Place those expressions in the shared review queue (`queues/uk.md` in the learner data), then revisit at most one or two in
a later lesson where they fit naturally, using changed details. Continue through
the roadmap instead of waiting for perfection.

For example, origin and languages belong in Session 1, liking in Session 2, time
forms in Session 3, and destination questions in Session 4. Do not insert an
unrelated review item merely because its calendar date has passed.

## Other tracks

This track is one source among several. Other Ukrainian tracks (grammar
books, courses, media) are separate tracks with their own sequence and
tracker; they meet this one only in the shared review queue.

- Give every lesson and practice-history entry its track's source label.
- Preserve this track's session numbering and history.
- A later track may revisit a weak expression from this one; the queue keeps

## Session 0 — Diagnostic conversation

### What the learner should be able to do

- Recognize a greeting and respond appropriately.
- Introduce himself and give a few basic personal facts.
- Say which languages he speaks and how well.
- Understand simple questions containing хто, що, де, куди, коли, скільки and
  чому.
- Ask for repetition, slower speech, clarification, or an English explanation.
- Form a basic affirmative answer, negative answer, and question.
- Follow a short exchange without receiving an English translation first.

### Useful repair language

- Я не розумі́ю.
- Повторі́ть, будь ла́ска.
- Говорі́ть пові́льніше, будь ла́ска.
- Що це означа́є?
- Як це сказа́ти украї́нською?
- Я тро́хи говорю́ украї́нською.

### Exit criterion

Complete one usable guided conversation of at least three minutes. Record weak
or untested items for later review, mark the diagnostic **Revisit** when gaps
remain, and continue to Session 1. The diagnostic does not block progression.

## Session 1 — Introductions and conversational control

### What the learner should know

- Formal and informal greetings and farewells.
- Names, nationality/origin, residence, work, and languages.
- ти versus ви in common interactions.
- Personal pronouns and present-tense forms needed for basic self-description.
- Basic question formation using intonation and question words.
- The repair expressions from Session 0 without prompting.

### Staged lesson parts

#### Session 1A — First meeting

Teach and practise only:

- A formal greeting and `Ду́же приє́мно.`
- `Мене́ зва́ти…`
- `Я з…, але́ живу́ в…`
- The reciprocal question `А ви?`
- `Повторі́ть, будь ла́ска.`

Use the first five relevant lines from the authoritative Session 1 opening as
the active phrase set. The complete opening is still presented first, but later
lines are exposure only and must not become surprise production tests.

#### Session 1B — Work, languages, and conversational control

After Session 1A, teach and practise occupation, named languages and
proficiency, reasons for learning, three relevant questions, and a second
independent repair strategy.

### Speaking tasks

- Part A: meet someone, exchange names, state origin and residence, ask
  `А ви?`, and request repetition once in a genuine repair moment.
- Part B: extend the introduction to work, languages, and reasons for learning;
  ask three relevant questions and use a second repair strategy.

### Coverage criterion

Mark Session 1 **Covered** once the accumulated transcripts include greetings,
name, origin, residence, occupation, languages, relevant questions, and repair
expressions. Modelled and cued material counts as coverage. Keep individual
expressions needing another turn in the shared review queue (`queues/uk.md` in the learner data) without repeating
Session 1.

## Session 2 — Food, shopping, numbers and payment

### What the learner should know

- Ask for and offer food and drinks.
- Express wanting, not wanting, having, needing, liking, and preferring.
- Use polite requests with будь ласка and an appropriate request form.
- Understand and produce the numbers needed for prices and quantities.
- Ask how much something costs and understand a simple price.
- Order, change an order, pay, and check whether something is available.
- Use the most frequent object forms in learnt phrases, without needing to name
  the grammatical case.

### Core patterns

- Я хо́чу… / Я не хо́чу…
- Я хоті́в би… / Я хоті́ла б…
- У вас є…?
- Да́йте, будь ла́ска…
- Скі́льки це ко́штує?
- Мо́жна…?
- Мені́ потрі́бно…

### Speaking tasks

- Order a drink and a small meal.
- Buy two items, understand the total, and pay.
- Respond when the requested item is unavailable.

### Coverage criterion

Complete both a café and a shop practice sequence. Record the food, drink,
quantity, price, availability, request, and payment vocabulary encountered and
the learner's sentence attempts. Place incomplete or corrected patterns in
the shared review queue (`queues/uk.md` in the learner data).

## Session 3 — Time, invitations and arrangements

### What the learner should know

- Ask and tell the time.
- Understand today, tomorrow, yesterday, morning, afternoon, evening, now,
  early, and late.
- Use days of the week and practical calendar expressions.
- Ask when someone is free.
- Invite, accept, refuse politely, and suggest another time.
- Express ability and possibility with common forms of могти and можна.
- Use simple future intentions and relevant present-tense forms in conversation.

### Core patterns

- Котра́ годи́на?
- О котрі́й годи́ні?
- Коли́ ви ві́льні?
- Ви мо́жете сього́дні?
- Ході́мо…
- На жаль, я не мо́жу.
- Мо́же, за́втра?

### Speaking tasks

- Arrange a meeting with two rejected time slots.
- Invite someone to eat or drink.
- Reschedule an appointment after a misunderstanding.

### Coverage criterion

Practise invitations, refusal, an alternative day, an alternative time, and a
final arrangement. Record every day/time expression and the sentences produced;
carry corrected forms into the shared review queue (`queues/uk.md` in the learner data).

## Session 4 — Places, movement and directions

### What the learner should know

- Ask where something is, where someone is going, and where someone came from.
- Distinguish practical uses of де, куди and звідки.
- Use common movement verbs in familiar phrases.
- Understand left, right, straight ahead, near, far, here, and there.
- Ask for and follow a short sequence of directions.
- Refer to common locations such as a hotel, station, restaurant, shop, centre,
  street, home, and work.
- Use в/у, на, до and з/із in familiar location and movement expressions.

### Core patterns

- Де знахо́диться…?
- Як пройти́ до…?
- Куди́ ви йдете́?
- Я йду / ї́ду до…
- Поверні́ть ліво́руч / право́руч.
- Іді́ть пря́мо.
- Це дале́ко?

### Speaking tasks

- Ask for directions and repeat them back.
- Explain how you are travelling somewhere.
- Correct a route after the assistant deliberately misunderstands the
  destination.

### Coverage criterion

Practise a three-step route and a second route with changed details. Record the
place, movement, direction, and transport vocabulary encountered together with
the learner's sentence attempts.

## Session 5 — People, possessions and daily life

### What the learner should know

- Talk simply about family, friends, colleagues, home, and work.
- Identify people and objects using це, цей/ця/це and basic descriptions.
- Express possession using мій/моя/моє/мої and the common у мене є pattern.
- Ask and answer who something belongs to.
- Describe a normal day with a small set of high-frequency verbs.
- Use basic present, past, and future references where needed for an everyday
  account.
- Keep adjective, possessive, and noun forms understandable in familiar phrases.

### Speaking tasks

- Describe two people and their relationship to you.
- Explain what you normally do in the morning and evening.
- Say what you did yesterday and plan to do tomorrow, using simple sentences.
- Ask the assistant corresponding questions.

### Coverage criterion

Record sentences about at least two people, a normal day, yesterday, and
tomorrow. Preserve corrected versions and carry any unfinished time or
possession patterns into the shared review queue (`queues/uk.md` in the learner data).

---

## Session 6 — Integrated conversation and final check

### What the learner should demonstrate

- Start, maintain, and close a conversation politely.
- Handle introductions, a transaction, a time arrangement, and directions in
  one continuous scenario.
- Ask relevant follow-up questions.
- Use Ukrainian repair strategies instead of immediately switching to English.
- Understand familiar material when wording differs from memorized phrases.
- Correct or reformulate an answer after a brief cue.
- Retain targets previously repaired in Sessions 1–5.

### Final scenario

The learner meets a Ukrainian-speaking visitor, makes a plan to go somewhere,
asks how to reach it, orders or buys something, changes the meeting time, and
ends the conversation. The assistant should introduce two small complications
without announcing them in advance.

### Coverage criterion

- Revisit each major topic from Sessions 1–5.
- Record at least one learner sentence from introductions, transactions, time
  arrangements, directions, and daily life.
- Preserve each corrected sentence alongside the learner attempt.
- Place expressions needing another turn in the shared review queue (`queues/uk.md` in the learner data).

Covering this session completes the planned review sequence. Vocabulary and
sentences in the shared review queue (`queues/uk.md` in the learner data) may still be revisited afterward.
